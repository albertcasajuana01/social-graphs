  • Week 5 · From language to numbers

    Wikipedia copies the paperwork, not the plot

    The Marvel pages get their text. We hunt for passages that appear word for word on two pages: they are publication histories, creator anecdotes and reading lists, almost never the fictional biographies. Every copied paragraph sits between pages that are already linked. Click an edge to read the copy.
  • Week 4 · Communities, consensus, weights & backbones

    Where did the Renaissance go? 200 Louvain runs and the philosophers no tradition will keep

    We switch to the philosopher network and run Louvain 200 times. Almost everyone keeps their tradition, except Galileo, Kepler, Bruno and the other Renaissance naturalists: a tight bloc that a different tradition absorbs each run. With weights they end up in Avicenna's group, and an interactive backbone lets you see it.
  • Week 3 · Paths, centrality & robustness

    Attack vs. random failure: how many heroes does it take to break the Marvel web?

    Removing characters by betweenness, by degree, and at random, and watching the giant component shrink — plus which single character fragments the network the most, and whether that's the same one betweenness would pick.
  • Week 2 · Random models & tail-shape

    Degree distribution vs. random models: heavy-tailed, BA-shaped, and not a clean power law

    Comparing the real Marvel graph against a matched random graph and a tuned Barabási–Albert model, then testing whether a strict power law is statistically supported.
  • Week 1 · Bootstrap edition

    Meet the network: 303 heroes, 17 loners, and one island of Morituri

    Loading the frozen week-1 snapshot, reconciling directed vs. undirected edge counts, mapping who's most linked-to vs. who links out the most, and finding a nine-node clique that Wikipedia forgot to connect to the rest of the Marvel universe.
